Learn Slurm in a day: DGX Spark + AGX Orin as a 2-node GPU cluster running a distributed PyTorch job

**Platform:** DGX Spark + AGX Orin via [Miramar Platform](https://miramar-labs-org.github.io/miramar-platform-gcp/)
**Repo:** [miramar-labs-org/slurm-lab](https://github.com/miramar-labs-org/slurm-lab) ·
**Full tutorial:** [Learning Slurm on a Two-Node Home Cluster](https://github.com/miramar-labs-org/slurm-lab/blob/main/docs/learning-slurm.md)

## Overview

Slurm schedules most of the world's supercomputers, and the only way to really learn it is to build a cluster.
I didn't have a rack, so I used what was on the desk: an NVIDIA **DGX Spark** (GB10, 128 GB unified) and a
**Jetson AGX Orin** (64 GB unified), connected by nothing better than home WiFi.

Two very different GPUs on a slow link make a bad training cluster, and that turned out to be the point.
Real clusters quietly provide identical nodes, a directory service, a shared filesystem and a fast
interconnect. I had to supply every one of those by hand, and each gap turned into a lesson.

## Approach

- **Slurm 23.11** from Ubuntu apt. `slurmctld` runs on the DGX, `slurmd` on both nodes, with munge auth, `cons_tres`,
  cgroup process tracking, and GPUs declared as typed GRES (`gpu:gb10`, `gpu:orin`). There's no accounting database.
- **Same world on both nodes:** the Orin's uid was renumbered to match the DGX (Slurm ships jobs by *number*),
  hosts are pinned by IP, a shared filesystem holds the only working copy of the repo, and the Python env sits at the same path on both nodes.
- **Exercises:** env and task layout, `squeue`/`scancel`, GPU-type targeting, job arrays, dependency
  pipelines, draining nodes.
- **Capstone:** PyTorch DDP across both nodes, launched by `srun torchrun`, with checkpointing and a mid-run
  `scontrol requeue` that resumes from the last checkpoint.

## Dataset

[tiny-shakespeare](https://github.com/karpathy/char-rnn) (1.1 MB of public-domain text, 65 characters),
used to train a 0.83M-parameter char-level GPT. The model is deliberately tiny. Gradients have to cross WiFi every step.

## Results

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Cluster | 2 nodes, 2 GPUs (GB10 sm_121 + Orin sm_87), 32 CPUs |
| Collective backend | **gloo**. NCCL can't initialize on Jetson (see below). |
| gloo all-reduce, 16 MiB over WiFi | ~2.5 s/iter (~6.3 MiB/s bus bandwidth) |
| DDP step time, 2 nodes | ~450 ms |
| Compute-only step time | ~17 ms (DGX), ~45 ms (Orin) |
| Share of each step spent communicating | ~90% |
| 300 steps, with a requeue at step 80 | resumed from the step-75 checkpoint, val loss 2.11 |

The things that actually broke, and why:

1. **NCCL won't start on the Orin.** Jetson's NVML returns `NOT_SUPPORTED` for `nvmlDeviceGetP2PStatus`,
   even for a GPU paired with itself, and NCCL treats any failure there as fatal before it even picks a transport.
   No environment variable gets around it, in any NCCL version up to 2.32. gloo works fine.
2. **Slurm ignored the Orin's GPU.** 23.11 drops a GPU declared without a device file, and the Orin's GPU
   isn't `/dev/nvidia0`. Listing the file descriptors a CUDA process holds showed the real one: `/dev/nvgpu/igpu0/ctrl`.
3. **CUDA worked over ssh but not under Slurm.** The controller sent the job its *own* numeric group
   list, which on the Orin lacked `video`, needed for `/dev/nvmap`. Fixed with `LaunchParameters=disable_send_gids`.
4. **Every job waited up to 30 s with `InvalidAccount`.** With no accounting database, 23.11's main scheduler
   fails an association check that the backfill scheduler skips. Fixed by running backfill every 2 s.
5. **DDP hung at startup.** Whichever node wins rank 0 advertises its hostname, and the DGX resolves its own name to `127.0.0.1`.
   Fixed with `torchrun --local-addr=<LAN IP>`.

Three of those five come down to one theme: **anything identified by a number or a name has to mean the same thing
on every node.** Uids, gids and hostnames were the recurring source of trouble.

## Next steps

- `slurmdbd` + MariaDB for `sacct` job history, and the proper fix for `InvalidAccount`.
- `ConstrainDevices=yes`, so jobs can only open their allocated GPUs.
- A small NVML shim on the Orin to get NCCL running, mostly out of curiosity. Over WiFi the link is the bottleneck either way.
